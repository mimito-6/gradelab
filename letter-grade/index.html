How the letter grade math works

1. Cutoffs are inclusive at the bottom

Each letter covers a band that starts at its cutoff and runs up to just below the next cutoff: an A- is 90.0% to 92.9%, a B+ is 87.0% to 89.9%. That is why 90.0% is an A- and 89.9% is a B+ — there is no rounding in between. The same rule holds on the straight scale: 90.0% is an A, 89.9% is a B.

2. The 0.1-point cliff

A tenth of a point can change the letter: 92.9% is an A- (3.67 points) and 93.0% is an A (4.0 points); 59.9% is an F (0 points) and 60.0% is a D- (0.67 points). This calculator shows the strict reading and does not round your percentage — if your teacher rounds 89.9% up to 90%, enter the rounded 90% and the letter follows.

3. Points are not proportional to the percentage

A 3-point gap in percentage is not worth the same everywhere: going from 90% to 93% (A- to A) gains 0.33 GPA points, but going from 87% to 90% (B+ to A-) gains 0.34 — and the jump from 59.9% to 60% (F to D-) gains 0.67 points for a tenth of a percent. The letter bands, not the raw percentage, decide the points, which is why two students 0.1 points apart can be a third of a GPA point apart.

4. A+ is worth the same as A

On the 4.0 scale, A+ and A are both worth 4.0 points — there is no 4.3. An A+ is a distinction on the transcript, not extra GPA. Some schools run a 4.3 or 5.0 weighted scale where an A+ does count for more; this tool uses the standard unweighted 4.0 scale, the same one as the GradeLab GPA calculator.

What this calculator cannot do

It cannot know your school's scale. The cutoffs here are the most common US ones (plus/minus: A at 93%, A+ at 97%; straight: A at 90%), but schools vary — some start A at 90% even with plus/minus, some at 94%. If your syllabus uses different cutoffs, the letter here is not your letter. Use the scale that matches your school, and when in doubt ask your teacher, not the calculator.

Frequently asked questions

What percentage is an A?

On the plus/minus scale this calculator uses, an A starts at 93% (97% and up is an A+). On the straight scale, an A starts at 90%. Both scales are common in US schools, so the number that counts is the one in your syllabus — this tool shows both scales so you can check either.

Is 89.9% a B+ or an A-?

A B+. Cutoffs are inclusive at the bottom: 90.0% is an A-, anything below it is a B+. That 0.1-point cliff is real on this scale — 92.9% is an A- and 93.0% is an A. Some teachers round 89.9% up; this calculator does not round, so it shows the strict reading. If your teacher rounds, the rounded number is the one to enter.

How many GPA points is each letter grade worth?

On the 4.0 plus/minus scale: A+ and A are both 4.0, A- is 3.67, B+ 3.33, B 3.0, B- 2.67, C+ 2.33, C 2.0, C- 1.67, D+ 1.33, D 1.0, D- 0.67, and F 0.0. There is no 4.3 — an A+ is worth the same 4.0 as an A. The straight scale is simpler: A 4.0, B 3.0, C 2.0, D 1.0, F 0.0.

What is the difference between the plus/minus and straight scales?

The plus/minus scale splits each letter into three bands (A-, A, A+), so the same percentage can map differently: 88.4% is a B+ (3.33 points) on the plus/minus scale but a B (3.0 points) on the straight scale. The plus/minus scale is the more common one in US colleges and is the default here; it is also the scale the GradeLab GPA calculator uses, so the two pages never disagree.

Why does 92.9% show A- when my school says it is an A?

Because scales vary by school: some schools start A at 90%, some at 93%, some at 94%. This calculator uses the most common US cutoffs (A at 93% on plus/minus, 90% on straight) and lets you switch scales. If your school's cutoffs differ, the letter it shows is not your school's letter — check your syllabus and use the scale that matches it.

How is this different from the test grade calculator?

The test grade calculator starts from correct and total questions (42 out of 50) and gives you the percentage and the letter. This page starts from the percentage itself (84%) and adds what the test page does not: the GPA points, the full percentage range of your letter, and exactly how many points you need for the next letter up.
